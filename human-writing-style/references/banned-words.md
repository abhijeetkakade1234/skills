# AI writing tells — word and phrase list

Grep a draft against this list before delivering it. Source: patterns cataloged in Wikipedia's `WP:AIWRITING`.

## Overused vocabulary (single words)

delve, crucial, pivotal, robust, intricate, tapestry, testament, underscore, bolster, foster, garner, showcase, align, enhance, elevate, leverage, unlock, unleash, navigate, realm, seamless, holistic, synergy, vibrant, profound, groundbreaking, meticulous, comprehensive, multifaceted, nuanced, myriad, plethora, boast(s), nestled, invaluable, indelible, transformative, revolutionize, empower, streamline, cultivate, embark, dive/dive-in, harness, unravel, illuminate, resonate, spearhead, catalyst, cornerstone, hallmark, ever-evolving, fast-paced

## Marketing verbs replacing plain copulas

"serves as," "stands as," "functions as," "operates as," "represents" (used for plain "is"), "offers" / "features" / "boasts" (used for plain "has")

## Padding sentence patterns

- "not just X, but also Y"
- "not X, but Y"
- "X, not Y"
- rule-of-three adjective/phrase triplets used reflexively
- "it's important/worth noting that"
- "it goes without saying"
- "needless to say"

## Undue significance / legacy framing

"marks a pivotal moment," "represents a significant shift," "underscores the importance of," "plays a vital/significant role in," "a testament to," "cements its place," "leaves a lasting/enduring legacy," "paves the way for"

## Weasel attribution

"industry reports suggest," "many experts believe/argue," "observers note," "some critics say," "it is widely believed," "studies show" (unnamed)

## Throat-clearing openers/closers

"In conclusion," "Overall," "In summary," "At the end of the day," "In today's fast-paced world," "In the ever-evolving landscape of," "Despite its [X], it faces [Y]" as a rote closing template

## Promotional / travel-brochure tone

"nestled in the heart of," "boasts a rich history/culture," "a vibrant hub/tapestry of," "steeped in tradition," "a hidden gem"

## Collaborative-address filler

"let's dive into," "we'll explore," "join us as we," "you might be wondering"

## Formatting tells

- Title Case headings in running prose
- bold applied to nearly every key term
- bullet lists of `**Label:** sentence` where the content is actually prose
- em dash used more than once or twice per document
- curly/smart quotes where the format expects straight quotes
- emoji used as structural markers instead of headings/lists
